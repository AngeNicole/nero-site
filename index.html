About NERO

How NERO began

A beautiful chair can look ordinary in the wrong light. NERO began with that thought.

Italian furniture houses have had decades to earn their names. NERO had none. So instead of borrowing a past, it set one rule it could keep from the first day: every piece is designed together with the light that shows it best.

Each collection ships with its own lamp. Each piece arrives with a note on where the lamp goes and where the shadow falls. The colours come from rooms in late afternoon, and the lineage is Italian modernism, the work of Gio Ponti and Carlo Scarpa, carried into rooms in Kigali and beyond.

The brief was simple to write and hard to meet: make a new house feel inherited.

The atelier

Who makes it

One designer, Ange Nicole Mukundwa, and three workshops: one for frames, one for covers, one for lamps. The materials change with every client, so the workshops are named for what they make, not what they make it from. This is the kind of work that happens there.

I wanted a house that could feel inherited on its first day. Light was the one thing it could own.Ange Nicole Mukundwa, founder
